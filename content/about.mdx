# About

I'm Harsh, a CS senior in Noida. I'm doing a B.Tech in Computer Science with a
specialization in AI and ML at Sharda University, and I graduate in June 2027.

## What I build

Most of my projects start because something annoyed me.

When I started learning Japanese, every site I tried drew the characters a
little differently, with dots on one and strokes on another for kana like shi
and tsu. That's fine if you already know the language. It wasn't fine for me. So I made
[Nihongo Path](https://nihongo.harshnarain.me), the course I wanted: kana up to
JLPT N4, with pronunciation written in Devanagari instead of romaji, and a
review system that brings words back just before you'd forget them. I revise
on it every day.

This summer I interned at Siddhi Sarees, on a team rebuilding their website. My
actual job was small fixes. But orders moved through WhatsApp chats, phone
calls and sticky notes, and the only people who could tell where an order
stood were the ones making the bills at the front desk. I felt overwhelmed
just imagining being in their place, so I built an
[order dashboard](https://siddhi.harshnarain.me) that nobody had asked for.
Looking back, it's a prototype: no login, no database, no way to edit an order
from the dashboard itself. The timeline and the metrics were the useful
part.

There's also [Budget Buddy](https://budget.harshnarain.me), which shows me
where my money actually went, and
[ShareTheMeal](https://sharethemeal.harshnarain.me), a small app for posting
leftover food for NGOs nearby.

## How I work now

I used to build things until they worked and stop there. Now I check load
times and image sizes, use SVGs wherever I can, and write comments and commits
that a new person can follow. I picked that up during the internship, reading
old code that someone had commented properly long before AI tools were around.

I'd rather read the documentation than follow a tutorial made for everyone.

## Research

I also do a bit of ML research. One project looks at bugs in training code
that leave accuracy looking normal. The other checks how much a popular
rare-disease benchmark overlaps with the answers it's graded against. The
first one ran mostly overnight on my laptop's RTX 3050.
